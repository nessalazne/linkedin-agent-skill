# Questions

The difference between a question that works and one that wastes the session:
a good one asks about **one moment**. A bad one asks about a **category**, and
gets a summary back. Summaries can't be posted.

## Opening

- "What have you been working on that you can't stop thinking about?"
- "What did you spend most of last week actually doing?"
- "When someone asks what you do, what do you say, and what do you leave out?"

Skip "tell me about your career". It gets a CV read aloud.

## Timeline

- "What were you hired to do there, and what did you end up doing?"
- "What changed between when you joined and when you left?"
- "Which month was that?" Ask it every time you hear *recently* or *a while ago*.

## Receipts

The hardest section to fill and the one drafts reach for most.

- "Put a number on that. A rough one is fine, just say it's rough."
- "What was it before, and what was it after?"
- "How long did it take, and how many people?"
- "What did it cost, in money or hours?"
- "How would you prove that to someone who doubted you?"

Skip "what were your key achievements". It gets adjectives.

## Shipped

- "What exists now that wouldn't exist if you hadn't been there?"
- "What do you point at when someone asks what you've done?"
- "Which part of it was yours?" This stops a post claiming a team's work.

## Turning points

- "What did you believe a year ago that you don't now?"
- "What did someone have to talk you out of?"
- "When did the plan change under you, and what did you do?"
- "What advice did you take that turned out wrong for you?"

## Scars

- "What's the most expensive mistake you've made at work?"
- "What did you ship that you wouldn't ship now?"
- "What do you check now that you never used to?" The scar sits behind the
  habit, and this gets there without asking for a confession.

One question here, then take what comes back.

## Positions

- "What do you think is true that most people in your field disagree with?"
- "What does holding that view cost you?" No cost, no position.
- "What advice does everyone give that you think is wrong?"
- "Who would argue with you about this, and what would they say?"

## Recurring stories

- "Which three stories do you already tell in person?"
- "What's the story you end up telling at every dinner?"
- "What do people ask you about more than once?"

## Naming and limits

Ask directly. Never guess.

- "Can I name that company in a post, or keep it anonymous?"
- "Who would be unhappy to see themselves mentioned?"
- "Is anything here under NDA or still in progress?"
- "What do you want kept out of your posts completely?"

## Post mode, in order

1. "When did this last actually happen to you?"
2. "Walk me through it. What happened first?"
3. "What's the number in this story?"
4. "What did you get wrong at the time?"
5. "Who disagrees with you on this?"
6. "What should someone reading this do differently tomorrow?"
7. Read the spine back in five lines. Keep their correction word for word.

## Questions that waste the session

| question | what comes back | why it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Tell me about yourself" | a CV | no moment to hang a post on |
| "What are your strengths?" | adjectives | everyone claims them |
| "What are your content pillars?" | marketing words | asks them to do the strategy |
| "What makes you unique?" | hedging | invites a performance |
| "Any other achievements?" | silence | closed, and puts the work on them |
| "Describe a challenge you overcame" | a job-interview answer | primes a rehearsed shape |

## When it gets hard

**They go quiet.** Usually they're remembering, not resisting. Wait. If it
lasts, drop to something small: "What did you do on Tuesday?"

**They talk for ten minutes.** Let them. The material is in the tangent more
often than in the answer, and a follow-up costs less than a new subject.

**They decline.** That line is closed. Write it under Off limits and tell
them you have.
